# Sanctions and offshore

Screen the company, its group, and the people in its leadership against sanctions lists, and look for it in the offshore leaks databases. Search the legal name, trading and former names, the parent and acquisition-company names in the entity record, and the company's people. Also search the most distinctive single word of each group-company name on its own (a holding vehicle's code name, such as "Falcon" in "Falcon Corporate Holdco, Inc."), since lists and leaks spell vehicles differently, and list under Facts every name and word you searched in each list, so the coordinator can tell which group names found later are already covered. Collect the people yourself before searching: the current directors and controlling persons from the company's register (for a UK company, the Companies House `/officers` and `/persons-with-significant-control` pages; for a US company, the state register's officers or managers), and the CEO, CFO, and chair from the company's leadership page.

## Where to look

- **OpenSanctions** combines the US, UK, EU, UN, and other sanctions lists with politically exposed persons (PEPs). With a key in the `OPENSANCTIONS_API_KEY` environment variable, call `https://api.opensanctions.org/search/default?q=<name>` with the header `Authorization: ApiKey <key>`, and keep the key out of your report. Without a key, the API returns 401: fetch the website search, `https://www.opensanctions.org/search/?q=<name>`, instead, with the web fetch tool only: its robots.txt bars scripts such as curl and mr.py from the search.
- **US Consolidated Screening List** (trade.gov), one free file merging the US government's sanctions and export lists: OFAC's SDN and non-SDN lists (including the Chinese Military-Industrial Complex list), and the Commerce Department's Entity List, Denied Persons, Unverified, and Military End User lists, and the State Department's ITAR debarments and nonproliferation sanctions. With Python, run `python <mr.py path> csl "<name>" "<other name>" ...` once with every company and person name (a few seconds; it downloads the 19 MB CSV and returns a `csl_match` for each entry whose name or alias contains every distinctive word of a name, with the list, programmes, addresses, identifiers, and listing date). Without Python, download `https://data.trade.gov/downloadable_consolidated_screening_list/v1/consolidated.csv` once and search it. Confirm a US sanctions hit on OFAC's own search, `https://sanctionssearch.ofac.treas.gov`; the old SDN CSV address at treasury.gov now redirects to `sanctionslist.ofac.treas.gov`, so follow redirects if you fetch it.
- **US lists the screening list leaves out**, searched by the company's and group companies' names: the UFLPA Entity List (`https://www.dhs.gov/uflpa-entity-list`, forced labour in China's Xinjiang region; its goods are barred from US import) and the Section 1260H list of Chinese military companies (`https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/search?conditions%5Bterm%5D=1260H`, the Defense Department's list, published in the Federal Register) when the group has a company, factory, or supplier in China or Hong Kong; the FCC Covered List (`https://www.fcc.gov/supplychain/coveredlist`, telecoms and video-surveillance equipment barred from US networks; it blocks scripts, so use a search restricted to fcc.gov) when the company makes such equipment; and SAM.gov exclusions (`https://sam.gov/search/?index=ex`, companies and people barred from federal contracts and grants; its API needs a key, so use the web search, in a browser tool if the fetch tool gets a blank page) when the group has a US entity.
- **ICIJ Offshore Leaks** (Panama Papers, Paradise Papers, Pandora Papers, and others). With Python, run `python <mr.py path> icij "<name>" "<other name>" ...` once with every company and person name: it returns an `icij_node` for each node whose name contains every distinctive word of a name, with the node type, the leak, its countries, its incorporation date and status where the leak gives them, and its page `url`, and drops the search's loose matches. A node with `extra_words` has words the name you searched lacks ("Vuppala Rao - Sameer" for "Sameer Rao"), so treat it as a different person or company until its details match. Without Python, the website search answers fetch tools with an empty bot-check page, so use its reconciliation API: POST `https://offshoreleaks.icij.org/api/v1/reconcile` with the body `{"queries":{"q0":{"query":"<name>"}}}`. Each result has an `id`, a `name`, the leak it came from, and a score; the node's page is `https://offshoreleaks.icij.org/nodes/<id>`, which you can open in a browser tool if you have one.

Financial regulators' own sanctions and enforcement (the CSSF, Banca d'Italia, the FCA, BaFin, and the like) belong to the National registers agent for that country, not to this source; leave them and say so under Gaps only if no register agent was sent.

## Checks

1. **Sanctions and export controls:** any listing of the company, a group company, or a named officer or controlling person on a sanctions, export-control, forced-labour, or procurement-exclusion list, with the list, the programme, the listing date, and the identifiers the list gives (country, registration number, date of birth month and year). Check each hit against the entity record's identifiers; a name match alone is a possible match, and goes under Gaps with its URL.
2. **PEPs:** any officer, director, or controlling person listed as a politically exposed person, with the position the list gives.
3. **Offshore leaks:** any node whose name matches the company, a group company, or a named person, with the leak, the node type (entity, officer, intermediary), its jurisdiction, and its linked entities. Check the match against the entity record before reporting it.

Report as red flags: a listing of the company, a group company, or a named officer or controlling person on any of these lists, including an export-control, forced-labour, or exclusion list (established, by the listing body's decision); and an appearance in an offshore leak that ICIJ's reporting ties to wrongdoing (an allegation). A PEP listing, or a leak appearance with no reported wrongdoing, goes under Facts: holding an offshore company is not itself wrongdoing.
