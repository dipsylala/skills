# National registers

Company registers outside the UK and US, for the countries where a muckraker target, its parent, or a subsidiary is most often registered. Run the section for each country the entity record names. To find which countries those are, a company's own published statements often list every subsidiary with its country: a modern slavery statement, a tax strategy, the subsidiaries note in the accounts, or a regulatory filing. For a country not listed here, find its registers in that country's section of Wikipedia's [list of official business registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_official_business_registers) (the page source, `?action=raw`, is plain text): the company register, and the insolvency and beneficial-ownership registers where the section lists them. Use the list only to find the registers; open each register itself and cite it, since the list is loosely sourced and some links are stale. When a link is dead, search for the register by name, or try OpenCorporates' list at `https://opencorporates.com/registers`. Use what each register shows free, and record the rest under Gaps.

Read the register's own page or API response, not an aggregator. Where a register answers fetch tools with a bot check or a form, open it in a browser tool if you have one; if the page still shows a verification check, leave it and move on. Failing that, or for a country whose register you cannot find, use a copy of the register. For European companies, North Data's company pages are free with no login and combine register entries, officers, filed financials, and events (insolvency, shareholder agreements) into one page: search `https://www.northdata.com/search?query=<name>` and open the matching company. For any country, use OpenCorporates' copy: `https://opencorporates.com/companies?q=<name>&jurisdiction_code=<country code>` and the company page it links to, or the API as [us-state-registry.md](us-state-registry.md) describes when `OPENCORPORATES_API_TOKEN` is set. Say which facts came from the copy, and record what it hides behind a login under Gaps. Leave paid documents and sign-in pages alone, and list them under Gaps with their titles.

## Records from mr.py

When your prompt gives `<cc>-<number>.jsonl` files, the coordinator has already read that country's register with `mr.py`: take the facts below from those records, citing their URLs, and do not query those APIs, notices, or filings again. Spend your searches on what each country's part says the records lack. A major `<cc>_flag` is a red flag: quote its `quote` where it has one and cite its `url`.

### France (`fr-<SIREN>.jsonl`)

The register profile (`fr_company`), officers and the statutory auditor (`fr_officer`), the register's published figures or a `fr_finances` record with `published: false` (accounts filed confidentially under art. L. 232-25, or not filed), and the BODACC notices as typed records: `fr_accounts_filed` (financial year end, confidential or not), `fr_capital` (each increase or reduction with the capital before and after: the capital-increase timeline for the funding cross-check), `fr_officer_change` (the notice's own wording), `fr_insolvency` (none means BODACC has none), and `fr_struck_off`. A `fr_flag` is a financial year whose accounts should have had a BODACC filing notice by now and have none. BODACC publishes about two million accounts-filing notices a year, confidential filings included, so a `fr_flag` means the accounts were probably not filed; INPI would confirm it but needs an account, and a change of year end can also produce one, so check the next filed year end before calling it unfiled. A `fr_flag` is a red flag when its year ended within the last three years, quoting the latest `fr_accounts_filed` record's year end as the evidence and citing the `fr_flag` URL; list older ones under Facts.

### Ireland (`ie-<CRO number>.jsonl`)

The CRO's open data: the profile (`ie_company`, with the last and next annual return dates and the name's effective date), each accounts filing with the days it took after its annual return date (`ie_accounts_filed`), and `ie_flag` records: an annual return overdue, or one filed more than 56 days after its annual return date. The CRO publishes accounts filings in yearly datasets that lag by a year or more, so when the `ie_summary` note says the profile's last return is newer than the filings, find that return's received date in a copy of the filing list. The open data names no officers: find them as the Ireland row says.

### Luxembourg (`lu-<RCS number>.jsonl`, with the extract `cdb-<RCS>.xml`)

The newest 12 quarterly files of the Centrale des Bilans open data, read for the company: each filing (`lu_accounts_filed`, with the deposit date against the deadline of seven months after the year end), each year's main figures (`lu_accounts_year`: total assets, equity, cash, debts, convertible loans, bank debt, debts to affiliates, net result, and turnover and staff costs where the profit and loss account is filed in full; abridged accounts leave turnover out), and `lu_flag` records for late accounts and negative equity at the latest year end. A late-accounts flag with `recent: true` and a negative-equity flag are red flags; quote the `quotes` text and cite the extract file. Years deposited before the oldest quarter searched are missing (`--quarters 24` reaches further back).

### Norway (`no-<organisasjonsnummer>.jsonl`)

Brønnøysund's open data: the Enhetsregisteret profile (`no_company`, with the bankruptcy and winding-up flags, the share capital, and the number of shares), current officers and auditor (`no_officer`, with each person's birth month and year), the last three years of filed figures (`no_accounts_year`, each with the link to the filed PDF, whose notes name the parent and group loans), and the announcements: each accounts filing with its receipt date against the deadline of seven months after the year end (`no_accounts_filed`), capital changes (`no_capital`), § 3-8 agreements with the counterparty they name, usually a shareholder (`no_agreement`), and every other notice (`no_notice`, with the text of officer and auditor changes from the last 24 months). `no_flag` records are bankruptcy, winding-up or compulsory dissolution (flags or announcements), negative equity at the latest year end, late accounts (`recent: true` when the year ended in the last three years), and accounts past their deadline and not filed; a `late accounts` flag is major only with `recent: true`. Shareholders are not open data: spend your searches on the owner, from a `no_agreement` counterparty, the parent named in the filed accounts' notes, or a copy of the shareholder register.

### Finland (`fi-<business ID>.jsonl`)

PRH's open data (CC BY 4.0): the YTJ profile (`fi_company`, with the trade register status, other and former names, and the Virre page), every registered notice with its date, type, and entry codes described (`fi_notice`, deduplicated by record number, each tagged with its topics: capital, board, auditor, accounts, insolvency), the periods of any accounts filed digitally (`fi_accounts_digital`, figures not parsed), and a `fi_summary` with the dates of capital events, board and auditor changes, and accounts registrations. Without `--fy-end`, the script takes the year end from any digitally filed accounts, or skips the deadline check. `fi_flag` records are bankruptcy, restructuring, or liquidation, removal from the trade register, accounts registered more than eight months after the year end, and accounts past that deadline and not registered. A capital increase with a share cancellation (`KOROI` with `OSMIT`) or a new option or convertible issue (`VAIPA`) after a valuation markdown suggests a recapitalisation, which is an inference until a source says so. The open data has no officer names, share capital amount, notice text, or shareholders: spend your searches on the officers and their entry months, the share capital, and the financial year (Virre in a browser, or a copy such as Asiakastieto), and the owners.

### Denmark (`dk-<CVR>.jsonl`)

The Business Authority's open filings index and the XBRL of the latest three annual reports (`--years` for more): each filing with its approval and publication dates against the ÅRL § 138 deadline and its documents, including the parent's group accounts filed with it when a parent consolidates the company (`dk_accounts_filed`; a `parent_group_accounts` link means the company has a parent), the figures for the company and, in a group report, the group (`dk_accounts_year`, `scope` entity or group; `reported` is an IFRS report's undimensioned figures, which are the group's when the report is consolidated), the reporting class, audit firm, and approval date (`dk_report_facts`), the executive and supervisory boards and auditors named in each report (`dk_officers`), and the notes on going concern, events after the year end, the parent and related parties, subsidiaries, security, and the auditor's remarks (`dk_notes`). `dk_flag` records are late accounts (major when more than a week late on a year ended in the last three years), accounts past their deadline and not published, a gap between filed periods, negative equity at the latest year end, a modified audit opinion, an auditor's remark on a breach of the Companies Act (such as a capital loss not dealt with), and a going-concern note (`severity: review`: read it; a material uncertainty, or reliance on a parent's letter of support, is a red flag). Every `dk_*` record carries `transport`: the index and reports are served over plain http only, so confirm a red flag from them on datacvr (in a browser tool, over https) before reporting it. The officers are those named at each year end: spend your effort on datacvr (the Denmark row) for the current status, the current officers and their history, the owners, and the capital increases.

### Israel (`il-<company number>.jsonl`)

The Corporations Authority's register as the Public Knowledge Workshop copies it each day (`https://next.obudget.org/get/org/company/<number>`): the profile (`il_company`: Hebrew and English names, type, status with `status_en`, sub-status, registration date, the violating flag, the last annual report year, registered address, objects, any Israeli government contracts or support received, and `copy_date`), and `il_flag` records: a violating company (מפרה, for unpaid annual fees or unfiled annual reports; major), a status other than active (major, or minor for a voluntary liquidation), a sub-status warning before strike-off, and an annual report more than a year behind (minor). The records are a copy: name them as one with `copy_date`, and confirm a red flag in the Authority's own search (its `register_url`, in a browser tool) before reporting it. Officers, shareholders, allotments, charges, and annual reports are in the paid company file: record them under Gaps.

### Spain (`es-<CIF>.jsonl`)

The company's acts in BORME section A, read from the official PDFs (`mr.py es`): one `es_entry` per published inscription, with its sheet (hoja), inscription number and date, the acts (constitution, appointments and removals, capital increases and reductions, articles changes, change of address, dissolution, extinction), the officers named (`officers`, each with its act and role), and the capital changes with the resulting subscribed capital (`capital`: the funding cross-check; nominal amounts only, so compare the percentage increase in nominal capital with the round's size against its post-money valuation). The `es_summary` gives the current capital and the current officers worked out from the appointments and removals. `es_flag` records are a dissolution, extinction, insolvency proceeding, or provisionally closed register sheet (major: a sheet is closed, among other reasons, when accounts go undeposited), and inscription numbers missing from the PDFs read (minor: the PDF list is incomplete, so the current officers may be wrong). `es_notice` records are section C announcements naming the company (liquidation balances, mergers, capital reductions), from a daily scan. A corporate officer (a company as consejero) is listed with its natural-person representative (`REPR.143 RRM`): record both and pass both to the sanctions screening. Officers' names come from the PDF text layer, which sometimes runs two words together. BORME has no accounts deposits: the years deposited come from copies (empresia, einforma), which disagree, and the deposit dates only from the paid Registro Mercantil record, so never report a missing or late year from copies alone.

### Brazil (`br-<CNPJ>.jsonl`)

The Receita Federal's CNPJ open data as minhareceita.org serves it (`mr.py br`): the profile (`br_company`: legal and trade name, legal form, size, head office or branch, registration status with its date and reason, special situation, start date, capital, activity, city, and the month of the data), each partner and administrator in the QSA (`br_partner`, with the role, entry date, the country of a foreign partner, and a foreign partner's legal representative in Brazil; a foreign company partner is usually the parent), and `br_flag` records: a status other than ATIVA (BAIXADA, INAPTA, SUSPENSA, NULA) and a special situation (recuperação judicial, falência, liquidação), both major.

## Checks

For each country the entity record names:

1. **Profile:** registered name and number, legal form, status, incorporation date, registered address.
2. **Officers:** directors, managers, or board members, with appointment dates where shown.
3. **Accounts:** the latest filed accounts' period, filing date, and URL, and the headline figures where the register gives them as data (revenue, profit or loss, equity, debt, auditor). Do not download or read the accounts documents themselves: the Financials agent does that.
4. **Insolvency:** any insolvency, liquidation, or winding-up notice, with its date and type.
5. **Courts:** where the country's row names a court or regulator search, run it for the group companies' names; otherwise say under Gaps that the country's courts were not searched. The Courts agent searches only US and UK courts.

For each group company that a financial regulator licenses (an e-money, payment, or investment firm, or a bank), also check that regulator's published sanctions and enforcement (the CSSF in Luxembourg, Banca d'Italia and CONSOB in Italy, the FCA in the UK, DNB and the AFM in the Netherlands, BaFin in Germany), as the country's row says: the Sanctions and offshore agent covers sanctions lists, not regulators. Where two copies of register data disagree (on capital, the last accounts year, or headcount), report both under Facts and take capital history only from a register record.

Report as red flags, established by the register: an insolvency, liquidation, or winding-up proceeding, a status other than active (struck off, dissolved, in liquidation), and a negative equity figure in the latest filed accounts, each only for a company that a register, GLEIF, or the accounts show belongs to the group. A company linked to the group only by press, such as an acquisition that may have been an asset purchase, goes under Facts with what links it.
