# US state registry

Each US state keeps its own register of companies and LLCs, usually run by the Secretary of State. For a private US company this is the main public record: status, formation date, registered agent, and often the officers, managers, or members. A company formed in one state (commonly Delaware) registers as a foreign entity in each other state where it does business.

## Access

- **Start with the open state data.** Run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py us-states "<name>" ["<other name>" ...]` with the legal name and any other names in the entity record (a US subsidiary such as "<name> Holdings" or "<name> US"; leave off "Inc." and other suffixes, since it matches names that start with each one). It searches the registers that Colorado, New York, Texas, Oregon, and Connecticut publish as open data, in a few seconds, and returns each matching entity with its number, type, status, and, outside Texas, its state of formation (`formed_in`). A foreign registration in any of them names the home state, which usually answers where the company was formed when that state's own search is closed to you. Texas's franchise tax list often holds an overseas company's US subsidiary, because remote staff there make it liable. Each record gives the dataset query as `url` (cite it) and the register's own page where one exists. Match each entity to the company by address, state of formation, and agent, since the search returns every name that starts the same way. New York, Texas, and Oregon list only active entities. A Texas `status` other than `active` is a right-to-transact code to check on the Comptroller's taxable entity search before reporting it. A Texas record with no `number` is a franchise taxpayer not registered with the Texas Secretary of State, common for a foreign company with remote staff there, not a sign of a problem. Without Python, query the same datasets with the web fetch tool, for example `https://data.texas.gov/resource/9cir-efmm.json?$where=upper(taxpayer_name) like 'ACME%'`.
- **The state of formation from SEC.** A US company that raised money privately usually filed a Form D, which gives its jurisdiction and year of incorporation. When your prompt gives `formd.jsonl`, take them from its `formd_filing` records (`jurisdiction`, `year_of_inc`) and do not search Form D again; otherwise fetch `https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22<name>%22&forms=D` with the web fetch tool, and the filing's `primary_doc.xml` for those fields.
- **Then each state's own register.** Search the state's business entity search by name or entity number where it allows that, for the officers, filings, and status the open data leaves out. Most are free web pages. Some sit behind a CAPTCHA or bot check (California's bizfileonline.sos.ca.gov, for example) or answer 403; leave those alone, take what the open data and Form D give, and list the register under Gaps for the user to search by hand.
- **Delaware.** Its entity search says it forbids automated tools, so do not use it. Take the Delaware entity's existence and file number from other states' foreign registrations, Form D, or the company's own terms, and list the Delaware search under Gaps. Its free search shows only name, number, formation date, and registered agent in any case; status and filings cost a fee.
- **OpenCorporates.** It copies the state registers and links each foreign registration to the home registration. With a token in the `OPENCORPORATES_API_TOKEN` environment variable, use the API (`https://api.opencorporates.com/v0.4/`) with `api_token=<token>` as a query parameter, and keep the token out of your report: `/companies/search?q=<name>&country_code=us`, `/companies/us_<state code>/<entity number>` (officers, filings, branches), and `/officers/search?q=<person>&country_code=us`. Without a token, try one company page (`https://opencorporates.com/companies/us_<state code>/<entity number>`); if it answers 403, do not try others. Its website hides officers, incorporation dates, and filing details behind a login; record those under Gaps. Record which source, the state register, the open data, or OpenCorporates, each fact came from.
- The company's own terms, data processing agreement, and privacy policy name the entity that contracts with US customers (a terms page is often named after it, such as `/general-terms-<company>-inc-<date>`). Read a PDF version with `mr.py pdf-text <url>`.
- **Owners.** US registers do not name shareholders, and FinCEN's beneficial ownership register is not public (US companies were exempted from reporting to it in 2025), so do not look for it; the owners come from Form D's related persons, the company's own statements, and the Startup agent's funding records.
- **When every route is blocked**, stop after one try at each and record each under Gaps. Report `STATUS: FAILED` only when `mr.py us-states` and Form D also found nothing; an entity found only in the open data is `STATUS: FOUND`, with the checks it could not cover under Gaps. Before stopping, try the city's open business-registration data where the company has an office, which names the legal owner of a business location: for San Francisco, `https://data.sf.gov/resource/g8m3-pdis.json?$where=upper(ownership_name) like '%<NAME>%'`. It is a city record, not a state register, so say so.

## Checks

1. **Profile:** registered name, entity number, entity type (corporation, LLC, partnership), status, formation date, state of formation, principal and mailing addresses, registered agent.
2. **Status:** a status other than active or good standing: suspended, forfeited, revoked, delinquent, or administratively dissolved. In California, "Suspended - FTB" means the Franchise Tax Board suspended the entity for unpaid tax or unfiled returns.
3. **Officers and managers:** the people the latest annual report or statement of information names (officers, directors, managers, managing members), with the filing date. Note a filing that is overdue.
4. **Filing history:** formation, amendments, name changes, mergers, conversions, and the dates of annual reports or statements of information.
5. **Foreign registrations:** the other states where the entity is registered, with each one's number and status.
6. **Liens:** where the state's UCC search is free, any UCC financing statements naming the company as debtor, with the secured party and date. They show who has lent against its assets.
7. **Officers' other companies** (with an OpenCorporates token): run the officer search for each officer or manager, and record their other US companies with each one's status. Match on name and state together, since common names return many people.

Report as red flags, established by the register: a suspended, forfeited, revoked, or administratively dissolved status, in any state, that is current or began in the last three years (an older lapsed foreign registration since replaced or no longer needed goes under Facts); and an officer whose other companies were repeatedly dissolved by the state (administrative dissolution, forfeiture, revocation) rather than voluntarily. An overdue annual report or statement of information goes under Facts.
