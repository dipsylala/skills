# Press

Find what has been reported about the company, with the emphasis on trouble.

## Checks

1. **Overview:** search the legal name and trading name for recent coverage (last 3 years) and note the main events: acquisitions, leadership changes, launches, layoffs, and funding rounds missing from the facts file (do not search again for the rounds it lists).
2. **Adverse coverage:** run one search per term, the name together with that term alone, since combined OR queries return results for only the strongest terms: lawsuit, sued, fraud, investigation, regulator, fine, penalty, settlement, whistleblower, data breach, recall, sanctions, bankruptcy, administration, liquidation, layoffs, scandal, complaint. Include an **Adverse terms** table in your report with all 18 terms, each with its result: the URLs of relevant hits, or "no relevant results".
3. **Leadership:** confirm the current CEO and chair on the company's own leadership or about page, since it changes before aggregators catch up. Note an interim or acting title, and any CEO, CFO, or chair who left in the last two years; search `"<name>" "<company>"` with steps down, departs, and replaced for the reason they left. Then search the current CEO and chair, and those who left, with these terms, one search per term: lawsuit, fraud, investigation, settlement, fired. Keep to their role at this company or at earlier companies they led.
4. **Layoffs:** search layoffs.fyi (`https://layoffs.fyi`) and the press for the company's layoffs in the last three years, with the date, number or share of staff, and teams affected. For each US state where the company has an office, other than California and Texas (the coordinator's `mr.py warn` covers those), search that state's WARN notice page for the company. Leave the company's own hiring figures to the coordinator.
5. **Trade and local press:** for each serious item, look for the trade publication or local paper that covered it first; it often has the detail national outlets drop.

## Weighing sources

- Prefer original reporting (the outlet that broke the story, with named sources or documents) over aggregators and rewrites. Cite the original.
- Treat press releases and sponsored content as the company's own claims.
- Follow a story to its end where you can: an allegation later dropped, settled, or upheld changes its status.
