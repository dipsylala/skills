# Company profile

What the company says about itself, and what public profiles say about it. The company's own website is the most current source for leadership; LinkedIn has no public API, puts most pages behind a login, and usually answers direct fetches with HTTP 403, so it is a fallback.

## Checks

1. **Leadership:** open the company's leadership, management, or about page (the facts file gives its URL and the main names) and record every executive and title it lists, and any board members. Note interim or acting titles. Then search `"<company name>"` together with CEO, founder, CFO, and director, restricted to linkedin.com, and note any name or title that differs from the company page. Record names and titles only, for the coordinator to compare with registry filings.
2. **Profile:** from the company's about page and its LinkedIn company page (search `"<company name>"` restricted to linkedin.com if the page will not open), record the stated industry, headcount or headcount band, headquarters, founded year, and website. Check that the website matches the entity record.
3. **Consistency:** note where these disagree with the entity record or with each other (a different headquarters or founding year, a headcount out of line with the company's size, a leader listed on LinkedIn but not on the company page).
4. **Newsroom:** open the company's press or newsroom page and record announcements from the last 12 months about leadership, acquisitions, restructuring, or financing. Treat them as the company's own claims. Events the facts file already lists need only the company's own announcement, not the press coverage, which is the Press agent's; leave revenue and loss figures to the Financials agent.
5. **Hiring signals:** open the company's careers page; if it lists nothing, search `"<company name>" jobs` restricted to linkedin.com or use public job boards. Note whether the company appears to be hiring and in which locations, as a sign of activity.
6. **Product footprint:** take the company's domain from its own site, footer, or GitHub links rather than guessing `<name>.com`, which is often parked or owned by someone else. Then record:
   - the company number in the site footer or legal page, and whether it matches the entity record;
   - the domain's registration date, the site's history, and for a software company its GitHub organisation (take the organisation from the site's own GitHub link). With Python, run `python <mr.py path> footprint <domain> [--github <org>] [--founded <year the company claims>]`: `footprint_domain` gives the registration date and registrar (from RDAP, or the registry's WHOIS server for domains with no RDAP service, such as .io); `footprint_wayback` the first and last archived copy and, by year, the months with a copy (`months_ok` counts copies that loaded; captures with none that loaded are usually redirects to another address; captures from before the domain's current registration go in `prior_owner_captures`, an earlier owner's site, and are left out of `first_capture` and the founding check); `footprint_github` the organisation's created date, last push, repos pushed in the last six months, and stars of its own repos; and `footprint_flag` when the claimed founding year is more than two years older than both the domain and the first copy. Add `--linkedin <company page URL>` with the LinkedIn company page the company's own site links (footer or social icons), never one found by search, which can be another company with the same name: `linkedin_snapshot` gives, for the latest Wayback capture of each month (up to 24), the employee count LinkedIn showed, followers, size band and headquarters, with the capture's address as `url`; `linkedin_summary` gives the change in employees between the first and last capture with a count, and how many captures were login walls. The count is LinkedIn members who list the company, not payroll; small and new companies often have no readable capture, which `captures_listed` or `readable` of 0 shows. Without Python, use RDAP (`https://rdap.org/domain/<domain>`, or port-43 WHOIS such as `whois.nic.io` for .io), the Wayback Machine (`https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=<domain>&fl=timestamp,statuscode`; retry on HTTP 504), and the GitHub API (`https://api.github.com/orgs/<org>` and `/orgs/<org>/repos?sort=pushed`).

   Report as red flags: a site company number that does not match the entity record; a history the company claims (a founding year, "in business since") more than two years older than both its domain's registration and the site's first Wayback capture, with no earlier domain found; and a company domain now parked or for sale. A domain registered within two years of the founding date is normal and goes under Facts. Product activity that has stopped (no GitHub push or site change for over six months) goes under Facts. So does the LinkedIn employee trend, with the first and last capture's dates and counts, and the headcount the company claims beside it; a fall of 10% or more a year is for the coordinator to weigh against layoffs and growth claims, not a red flag on its own.
7. **Leaders' other roles** (US companies): LittleSis, a free database of US boards, executives, and their links, has an open JSON API (CC BY-SA). Search `https://littlesis.org/api/entities/search?q=<company name>`, then read `https://littlesis.org/api/entities/<id>/relationships?category_id=1` for positions at the company. For each executive and director listed, record their other current board seats and executive roles from their own relationships. Record them under Facts, for the coordinator to compare with the other sources.
8. **US imports** (companies that make or sell physical goods): ImportYeti shows US customs sea-shipment records by importer. Open `https://www.importyeti.com/company/<slug>` (take the slug from a search restricted to importyeti.com) in a browser tool; fetch tools get a bot-check page, and if the browser also shows a verification check, record it under Gaps. Record the last shipment date, total shipments, the shipments-over-time trend, and the main suppliers and their countries. A sharp fall or a stop in shipments goes under Facts, as a sign of trading activity.
