# Financials

Read the company's own financial statements and turn them into a multi-year table the coordinator can compare and reason about. The other registry agents report filing health; this source reads the numbers.

## Where the numbers are

- **US public filers:** `mr.py edgar-facts <CIK>` returns revenue, gross and operating profit, net income, cash, short-term investments, long-term debt, equity, and operating cash flow for the last five fiscal years, from the company's XBRL filings, with the latest restated value for each year. A `no_xbrl_financials` record means the CIK has never filed tagged statements. Without the script, the same data is at `https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK<10-digit zero-padded CIK>.json`; use the annual (`10-K` / `20-F`, `fp: FY`) values. Open the latest annual report for the notes listed in check 3.
- **UK companies, with `uk-accounts.jsonl`:** when your prompt gives this file, the coordinator has already read the iXBRL accounts with `mr.py uk-accounts`. Take the multi-year table and derived figures from its `uk_accounts_year` and `uk_accounts_derived` records, citing their `sources` URLs; figures carry the sign the accounts print. Take check 3's parent chain, going concern, events after the year end, and borrowing from its `uk_accounts_notes` sentences, and open the accounts only for a note those sentences leave unanswered (covenants, impairments, related-party balances). Say in the report where a `sign_mismatch` shows the filer's XBRL tag disagreeing with the printed sign. For a `uk_accounts_scanned` filing you need, run `mr.py uk-pages <its url> <folder> --pages 1-4 --scale 3` first, read the contents page, then run it again with `--pages` for just the primary statements and the notes you need (going concern, debt, events after the year end, related parties), and read those. Image n is page n. Each page image costs as much as several pages of text, so keep to a budget: about 15 pages per set of accounts, and two sets at most (the latest two cover four years through their comparatives; open an older set only for a year they leave out). Use `--scale 3` (smaller images, still legible for most statements) and switch to `--scale 2` only for a page whose figures you cannot read. Never render a whole set with no `--pages`: in one run that cost about 150k tokens for 70 pages. List a filing under Gaps as `needs OCR` only when its pages could not be read.
- **UK companies, without it:** open the latest two sets of accounts from the filing history (`https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/<n>/filing-history`; each accounts entry links to `/document?format=pdf`). Accounts filed electronically also have an iXBRL version with a text layer, at the same link with `?format=xhtml`; read that first, since the PDF is often an image. For a group, use the group accounts filed by the top UK company. Each set gives two years, so two sets give three or four.
- **UK listed companies:** large companies often file their accounts at Companies House as image-only PDFs with no iXBRL version. Use the annual report PDF from the company's investor site, or its ESEF iXBRL filing on the FCA National Storage Mechanism (`https://data.fca.org.uk/#/nsm/nationalstoragemechanism`), and the latest interim results for the current year.
- **French companies, with `fr-<SIREN>.jsonl`:** the coordinator has already read the French register and BODACC with `mr.py fr`. Take the published figures from `fr_finances` records, and the filing history from `fr_accounts_filed` (year end, confidential or not) and `fr_flag` (years with no filing notice). A `fr_finances` record with `published: false` means the register has no figures: say so under Gaps and do not query the French register, BODACC, INPI, or Pappers again.
- **Irish companies, with `ie-<CRO number>.jsonl`:** take the filing history from its `ie_accounts_filed` and `ie_flag` records. The accounts themselves cost money at the CRO: list them under Gaps, and take figures only from a press report of the filed accounts, citing the report.
- **Luxembourg companies, with `lu-<RCS>.jsonl`:** take the multi-year table from its `lu_accounts_year` records and the filing history from `lu_accounts_filed` and `lu_flag`, citing the saved extract (`cdb-<RCS>.xml`) and checking quotes against it with `mr.py quote-check <extract> "<quote>"`. Equity below zero, cash beside debts due within a year, and convertible loans are the figures to set against the funding story.
- **German companies:** the Unternehmensregister lists the filed accounts free, but opening one needs a CAPTCHA. Small companies (§ 267 HGB) publish only a balance sheet and notes, with no profit and loss account: take the year's result from the equity section (Jahresfehlbetrag or Jahresüberschuss, or the change in the loss carried forward). Map balance-sheet figures to their labels by checking that the totals tie. A free copy of the published accounts from a company-data site (craft.co, for example) may be used: name it as a copy. Leave paid copies (unternehmen24, North Data's figures) alone and list them under Gaps.
- **Dutch companies:** filed accounts (jaarrekening) cost money at KvK, and small companies publish only a balance sheet. Liza.nl (`https://www.liza.nl/en/<KvK number>/<name>`) shows free each filed year's balance sheet total, equity, debts, and FTE: use them as a copy, named as such, and take the year's result from the change in equity, saying it assumes no capital movement. The FTE figure covers that legal entity only. When the register names a foreign parent (a US "Holdings, Inc." above a Dutch B.V. is common for venture-backed companies), say under Gaps that the group's figures are not published.
- **Other jurisdictions:** use the register for that country in [national-registers.md](national-registers.md), Accounts column, where it publishes accounts free. Record under Gaps any that charge or need a login.
- **Scanned accounts:** filings with no iXBRL version are often image-only PDFs. Read UK ones through `mr.py uk-pages` as above, contents page first; for others, read them if you have an OCR tool. Otherwise list each one under Gaps as `needs OCR` with its URL, and take what figures you can from the prior-year comparatives in the next year's accounts.
- **No filed accounts** (a private US company, for example): record figures the company or reputable press have stated, each marked as a company claim or press report, and say under Gaps that no audited figures exist. Lenders' loan marks and credit ratings belong to the Debt and valuation marks and S&P Global sources; leave them to those agents.

## Checks

1. **Multi-year table:** for each of the last 3–5 years: revenue, gross profit, operating profit or loss, net profit or loss, cash and short-term investments, total debt, net assets, operating cash flow, and average headcount. State the currency and year end, and leave a cell empty rather than estimate it.
2. **Derived figures:** revenue growth for each year, operating margin, the year-on-year change in cash and investments, and runway (cash and investments divided by the last year's fall in them, when they fell and operating cash flow was negative; a fall funded by capital spending or dividends out of positive operating cash flow is not burn, so record it without a runway). Show each calculation's inputs so the coordinator can check it.
3. **The notes that predict trouble:**
   - auditor and audit opinion, and any emphasis of matter or going-concern paragraph;
   - the directors' going-concern statement and the period it covers;
   - debt: lenders, amounts, interest terms, maturity dates, and covenants, with any breach or waiver;
   - events after the balance sheet date (layoffs, refinancing, acquisitions, disposals);
   - impairments and write-offs;
   - principal risks the directors name;
   - related-party transactions and amounts owed to or by the parent.
4. **Changes between years:** a change of auditor, of year end, or of accounts type (full to abridged, for example), and any restated prior-year figures.
5. **Group companies:** the subsidiaries and participations the accounts list, each with its country, registered number where given, and share held. Record them under Facts: the coordinator adds those outside the US and UK to the national registers search.

Report red flags here as established findings, since they come from the company's own filed accounts: a qualified or adverse opinion, material uncertainty on going concern, a covenant breach, debt due within 18 months without agreed refinancing, a runway (as check 2 defines it) under two years, or a large impairment.
