# US federal money, run by you

When the entity record has a US entity, run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py usaspending "<legal name>" "<US entity name>" ... --since <founding year>-01-01 > "<run folder>/usaspending.jsonl"` while the agents work, with the legal names (former names too; leave off "Inc." and other suffixes). It reads USAspending.gov's open API for the last five years (from `--since`, the founding year; loans are searched back to April 2020 in any case, so PPP loans show): the largest contracts, grants, direct payments, other assistance, and loans to recipients whose names contain every distinctive word of a name (`usa_award`, with the agency, amount, dates, description, and award page), each fiscal year's obligations for the recipients found (`usa_year`), and a `usa_summary` with each recipient's UEI and page, each agency's share of the listed awards, any truncated groups, and the names the search returned that did not match.

Check each recipient's page (address, parent) before counting it as the company. `usa_vehicles` lists the indefinite-delivery contracts (IDIQs) the company holds a place on, which carry no money themselves; a `usa_flag` marks a fiscal year with negative obligations (an agency took money back: open the largest award's transaction history to see whether it was a closeout, a cut in scope, or a termination).

USAspending leaves out Other Transaction agreements (DIU and other OT deals, often a defence startup's largest) and some SBIR awards, so add those from SBIR.gov's firm portfolio (`https://www.sbir.gov/portfolio/<id>`, which the Startup agent finds) and the company's contract announcements, and say which figures come from where.

In SKILL.md step 4, put the federal total and the largest agency under Key facts, and compare it with the revenue in Financials: a federal share of revenue above about a third is a concentration to state in Key facts, not a red flag. Loans include SBA's PPP and disaster loans; a PPP loan is a fact about 2020 and 2021, not income. If the script fails, say so under Gaps.
