# Muckraker

Given a company name, find out who the company is, what it has filed, what has been reported about it, and where the sources disagree. You are the coordinator: you pin down the entity, send one research agent per source, and write the dossier from their reports. Every fact in the dossier carries a citation.

## 1. Pin down the entity

Take the company name and anything else the user gave (country, website, ticker, company number). When the user names several companies, build one entity record per company and run every later step for each. Search the web to find the candidates.

- One clear match: record its legal name, jurisdiction, website, ticker, SEC CIK, UK company number, US state of formation and state entity number, and parent company, as far as you can find them. Confirm each registry identifier by looking it up in that registry and checking that the name it returns is the entity you mean (a CIK can belong to the UK parent rather than the US company); record the registered name beside the identifier. When a state register sits behind a CAPTCHA or forbids automated use (Delaware's does), run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py us-states "<name>"`: other states' open registers often hold the entity's foreign registration, which names its state of formation (see [us-state-registry.md](references/sources/us-state-registry.md)). Say in the record where the number came from; if nothing confirms it, record it as unconfirmed and leave it to the US state registry agent. Look the entity up in GLEIF (`python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py gleif "<legal name>"` to find the LEI, or `mr.py gleif --registered-as <national register number>` when the name finds none, then `mr.py gleif <LEI>` for the parent chain and subsidiaries; without Python, the API in [gleif.md](references/sources/gleif.md)) and record its LEI and the countries its parents and subsidiaries are registered in (a candidate whose `category` is `FUND`, or a `gleif_fund` record, is a fund vehicle such as an investor's sub-fund named after the company, not the company: leave it out); they decide which registers step 2 sends agents to. For a company registered in the EU, run `mr.py vat <country> <VAT number>` (VIES): most countries return the name the tax authority holds now, which catches a holding renamed since the brand was set (Satispay S.p.A. became Momentum S.p.A., while the press still says Satispay), and GLEIF's `other_names` keeps former names too. Run `mr.py gleif` for each acquired company's own name too ("Azimo" for the payments business a company bought): an acquired subsidiary, often a regulated one, keeps an LEI of its own that a search for the parent's name never finds. For an entity registered outside the UK, also search Companies House for its name (`mr.py ch-search "<name>"` when `COMPANIES_HOUSE_API_KEY` is set; otherwise `https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/search/companies?q=<name>`): add any UK company with the same directors and any overseas registration (an FC number, with its UK establishment) to the record, which brings in UK Companies House. A venture-backed company outside the US often sits under a US holding company ("<name> Holdings, Inc.", usually in Delaware) and contracts through a US subsidiary: search the company's press releases for "registered in the U.S. as" and its data processing agreement, terms, and subprocessor or affiliates list for the contracting entities and group companies (a subprocessor list often names every subsidiary with its address, more than the about page does) (terms pages are often named after the entity, such as `/general-terms-<company>-inc-<date>`; read a terms PDF with `mr.py pdf-text <url>`), and add any US entity to the record; for a Dutch company, also read the group heads named on its KvK product page (the Netherlands row in [national-registers.md](references/sources/national-registers.md)). Search the CEO's or a founder's name as well, since a group company can carry a name or address the company name never finds: in EDGAR full-text search for Form D (fetch `https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22<first%20last>%22&forms=D` with the web fetch tool; the filer named in each hit is a US company that raised money with that person as an officer, often the US parent of a "flip"), and in Companies House's officer search (`https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/search/officers?q=<name>`), which finds a UK subsidiary that a name search for a common word buries among hundreds of unrelated companies. When the company has an office in the US and no US entity is known, add the office's city and state to the record so the US state registry agent searches for the entity behind it. Leave a field empty rather than guess it. Until the user has given the SEC contact (step 2), reach sec.gov only through the web fetch and search tools: confirm a CIK by fetching `https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK<10-digit zero-padded CIK>.json` with the web fetch tool and checking its `name` field, which is the intended route here, not a workaround (the `browse-edgar` company page comes back blank through web fetch).
- Several plausible matches (different countries, a parent and its subsidiaries, a similar name): list them with what tells them apart and ask the user which one to research. Researching the wrong "Acme Ltd" wastes every agent you send.
- No match: tell the user what you searched and ask for more detail (country, website, company number) before going further.

Done when the user has confirmed one entity, or the search found exactly one and you have recorded its identifiers.

Ask every run, and use only what the user types in answer, or a value they typed earlier in this conversation that they explicitly choose to reuse; never a name or email you know from elsewhere. Put this question in the same message as any entity question from step 1. If the user answers the entity question and leaves this one unanswered, ask it once more on its own; if they pass over it again, treat that as declining. If the user declines, send those agents without a value; the brief tells them what to do instead. Your own sec.gov requests follow the same rule as the agents' (see the SEC bullet in [references/brief.md](references/brief.md)).

Record in the dossier's Gaps every register the entity is in that no source searched: outside the US and UK, any country's register the National registers agent could not reach, and every national court system (no source searches courts outside the US and UK); for a US entity, any state register the US state registry agent could not reach.

Done when the source list is fixed and the SEC contact has been given or declined, or the user has asked to skip SEC.

## 3. Send the agents

Create a run folder for each company outside the user's project: in the session's scratchpad or temp directory if the runtime names one, otherwise `<system temp>/muckraker/<company-slug>-<YYYYMMDD-HHMM>/`. Record its absolute path in your next message so it survives context compaction.

Some sources you run yourself with the script instead of sending an agent, as the sections below say: UK Companies House when you can run Python and `COMPANIES_HOUSE_API_KEY` is set (run it before sending the agents, since it decides who writes Financials), Financials too when the UK accounts are the only filed accounts, the national registers in the table below (France, Ireland, Luxembourg, Norway, Finland, Denmark, Israel, Spain, Brazil) whenever you can run Python (before the agents, since their records go into the prompts), Form D (`mr.py formd`) when you can give sec.gov the SEC `User-Agent` (before the agents, since its records go into their prompts), GLEIF ownership whenever you can run Python, US federal money (`mr.py usaspending`) when the entity record has a US entity, and Debt and valuation marks when you can also give sec.gov the SEC `User-Agent` (run those three while the agents work). Send one agent per other chosen source per company, all at once, in parallel. When the group has companies in more than three countries outside the US and UK, split National registers into one agent per two or three countries, each with its own report file (`national-registers-<cc>-<cc>.md`): one agent covering six countries took twice as long as any other. Each prompt contains:

- the absolute paths of [references/brief.md](references/brief.md), the agent's source file, and `<run folder>/facts.md`, with the instruction to read all three first;
- that company's entity record from step 1;
- the absolute path of its report file: `<run folder>/<source file name>`, for example `<run folder>/press.md`;
- the SEC `User-Agent` value, for the agents marked "Queries sec.gov" only;
- the absolute path of [scripts/mr.py](scripts/mr.py), the skill's fetcher for EDGAR filings, XBRL figures, lender and fund marks, and quote checks;
- for the Financials agent, the absolute path of `uk-accounts.jsonl` when you made one;
- for each agent the national-registers table names, the absolute paths of the `<cc>-<number>.jsonl` files you made (and Luxembourg's saved extract), with the instruction to take that country's register facts from them as national-registers.md's "Records from mr.py" says, and not to query that register's APIs, notices, or filings again;
- for the SEC EDGAR, US state registry, and Startup agents, the absolute path of `formd.jsonl` when you made it, with the instruction to take the Form D filings from it and not to search or open them again.

Each agent writes its full report to that file and replies with only its status line (the brief gives the format). Wait for the status lines; leave the report files unread until step 4. If an agent has not replied long after the others, stop it, record its source as `FAILED: no reply` under Gaps, and continue.

### The facts file, written by you

Agents in earlier runs spent much of their budget finding the same basics: four to six of them searched the same funding rounds, leadership page, and about page. Before sending the agents (after the scripts that run first), write `<run folder>/facts.md` with what step 1 and those scripts established, so each agent starts from it instead of finding it again. Spend at most three more lookups on it: the company's leadership or about page, one search for its funding rounds, and its careers page. Keep it under about 60 lines, one fact per line, each with its source URL, and no analysis:

- **Entities:** each legal entity in the entity record with its jurisdiction, register number, LEI, and the parent and subsidiaries known so far; former names.
- **People in company roles:** the CEO, CFO, chair, and founders, with their titles as the company's own page gives them.
- **Funding:** each round found so far, with date, stage, amount, and lead investors.
- **Footprint:** website, careers page, headquarters and other offices, and the headcount the company claims.
- **Financial year end:** the date each entity's financial year ends, from its register entry (Virre in Finland, for example) or its latest accounts, so that deadline checks use the right year end and not 31 December.
- **Known events:** layoffs, executive departures and appointments, acquisitions, and the company's headline revenue or ARR claim from the last three years that step 1 or the scripts already turned up, each with its date and the article or filing that reports it. Leave this empty rather than search for it: it stops agents chasing the same story, and the Press agent still looks for events missing from it. Leave out claims found only on an aggregator with no primary source (a layoff tracker, a funding database): agents treat this file as known, and one such entry in an earlier run was another company's layoff.
- **Not this company:** other companies with the same or a similar name that searches will turn up (a games company sharing the brand name, a fund or SPAC named after the same word), each with what tells it apart, so agents can drop their results at once.

Mark anything unconfirmed as such. Agents treat these facts as known: they cite them from the file's URLs when they use them, check and extend them only where their own source file asks for that (the Startup agent still owns funding, the Company profile agent leadership, and the Press agent events), and record any contradiction they find under Facts with both URLs. Only you write the file.

### UK Companies House, run by you

For each UK company in the entity record (the entity, a UK parent or a UK subsidiary), before sending the agents:

1. Run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py ch <company number> > "<run folder>/companies-house-<number>.jsonl"`. It returns the profile, officers, persons with significant control and their statements, charges (or a `ch_charges` record with count 0 when there are none, and a `ch_profile_mismatch` when the profile says none but the register lists some), insolvency cases, an overseas company's UK establishments (`ch_uk_establishment`), the latest filings, and `ch_flag` records: overdue accounts or confirmation statements, Gazette notices, and filings made after their statutory deadline, each with the rule it breaks.
2. Run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py uk-accounts <company number> > "<run folder>/uk-accounts.jsonl"` (append with `>>` for a second company). It returns each year's figures from the iXBRL accounts (signed as the accounts print them, with `sign_mismatch` where the filer's XBRL tag disagrees), the auditor and going-concern basis, year-on-year changes, `uk_accounts_notes` with the latest accounts' sentences on the parent chain, going concern, events after the year end, and borrowing, and `uk_accounts_scanned` (with its page count) for accounts filed only as a PDF, usually a scan. `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py uk-pages <its url> "<run folder>/pages-<made up date>" [--pages 1-5,33]` saves a scanned PDF's pages as PNG or JPEG images, one per page and half size by default, to open with the Read tool (each image costs as much as several pages of text, so read the contents page first and then only the pages you need, with `--scale 3`); `mr.py pdf-text <url>` prints the text of a PDF made by software, such as an electronically filed SH01 or confirmation statement. A `uk_accounts_latest_scanned` record means the newest accounts are scans, so the notes and latest figures come from older accounts.
3. Add the parent and controlling-party names in `uk_accounts_notes` to the entity record if they are new, before sending the agents.
4. Run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py ch-officers <company number> > "<run folder>/ch-officers-<number>.jsonl"`. For each current director it lists their other UK companies that went into liquidation, administration, receivership, or a voluntary arrangement, or were dissolved after one (`ch_officer_insolvent_company`, with the case types; a members' voluntary liquidation, the solvent kind groups use to close subsidiaries, does not count), and a `ch_flag` for a director of two or more such companies (`severity: major`, the "phoenix" pattern), of one (`minor`), or a match on the disqualified directors register (`major`). It reports company roles only.
5. Write `<run folder>/companies-house.md` from the `ch` and `ch-officers` records, in the brief's report format, working through the checks in [companies-house.md](references/sources/companies-house.md). Overdue items, Gazette notices, and late accounts (`severity: major`) made up to a date in the last three years are red flags, established by the register's own dates; list older late accounts under Facts. Late officer, PSC, confirmation-statement, and overseas-registration filings, and a `uk_psc_mismatch` from `uk-accounts` (the PSC register says no one controls the company while its accounts name a parent) (`severity: minor`) are register housekeeping: list them under Facts, and make them one red flag only as a pattern (`ch_summary` counts those in the last 24 months; several is a pattern). A late-accounts flag's deadline assumes no extension or first-accounts period, so say so, and pass on its `possible_extension` when it has one. Each red flag cites the record's URL and quotes its `quote` field (the filing as the website prints it, such as "31 Oct 2024 AA"). Take check 8's parent chain from `uk_accounts_notes`. A `ch-officers` major flag is a red flag quoting the companies' names and statuses; a minor one goes under Facts, with whether the director served in the year before that company's insolvency.

If `COMPANIES_HOUSE_API_KEY` is not set or the script fails, send the UK Companies House agent instead, and the Financials agent reads the accounts itself.

### Financials, run by you when only UK accounts are filed

When the entity has no tagged SEC financials (no CIK, or `mr.py edgar-facts <CIK>` returns `no_xbrl_financials`) and the entity record names no company outside the UK and US that files accounts, `uk-accounts` has covered every filed set of accounts. If it also printed no `uk_accounts_latest_scanned` record (the newest accounts have a text layer), write `<run folder>/financials.md` yourself from the `uk-accounts` records instead of sending the Financials agent, working through the checks in [financials.md](references/sources/financials.md): the multi-year table, the derived figures, the notes from `uk_accounts_notes`, and any `sign_mismatch` or `uk_accounts_scanned` records. Say under Gaps that group accounts are not published, and leave group figures from ratings or press to step 4. In every other case, including newest accounts that are scans, send the Financials agent with the path of `uk-accounts.jsonl`.

Do what the last column says, then give the file's path to the agents the table names, with the instruction in step 3. ### Form D, run by you

When the user has given the SEC contact, run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py --user-agent "<SEC User-Agent>" formd "<legal name>" "<former or US entity name>" "<founder's surname>" ... > "<run folder>/formd.jsonl"` before sending the agents (a few seconds per name). Search people by surname, or by the full legal name a Form D gives (filings write "Anthony John Piplica", so "AJ Piplica" finds nothing); the people found in a company's first Form D are the names to add for a second run. Leave out a common surname on its own ("Guez" returned 89 unrelated filings); search the full name instead. A company whose parent is incorporated outside the US (an Israeli Ltd, for example) usually raises through that parent and files no Form D, so finding none is normal there and says nothing about the US subsidiary; only a US parent (a "flip") files them for its rounds. It runs EDGAR full-text search for Form D filings naming each one and reads every filing found: the issuer, its state and year of incorporation, address, former names, industry, the amount sold and offered with the filer's note on them (`sales_clarification`, which often explains a gap with the announced round, such as SAFEs converted into it), the date of first sale, the number of investors, and the related persons with their roles (`formd_filing`, names and roles only, never their addresses), and a `formd_summary` grouping the filings by issuer. Add each issuer that is the company or a group company (match it by name, address, and related persons) to the entity record with its state of formation, and leave out `investor_vehicle` issuers (a pooled fund, or an issuer named "a Series of", SPV, or Fund, such as "<company> a Series of <platform> LLC"), which invest in the company; check any other issuer whose name is not the company's, such as a syndicate LLC, the same way. Give the file's path to the SEC EDGAR, US state registry, and Startup agents. If the user declined the SEC contact, skip this: the agents search Form D with the web fetch tool as their source files say.

### Layoffs and hiring, run by you

While the agents work, run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py warn "<legal or trading name>" > "<run folder>/warn.jsonl"` and `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py jobs <careers page URL> --history --offices "<office city>" "<metro area>" ... > "<run folder>/jobs.jsonl"`. `warn` returns US WARN layoff notices (filed for layoffs of 50 or more at one site) from the states that publish them as open data, California and Texas so far, and its `warn_summary` names the states searched: list the others where the company has a US office under Gaps unless the Press agent covered them. `jobs` finds the company's Greenhouse, Lever, or Ashby board, counts its open roles by department and location, and with `--history` counts them in up to 12 monthly archived copies of the board page (approximate; Ashby boards have no archived list). For Ashby and Lever boards, `jobs_posting_ages` tallies the open roles by the month they were posted: a proxy for recent hiring where no archived trend exists. A `jobs_board_guessed` record means the board was found from the domain name: check that its locations are the company's offices before using it. When the domain's name has boards on more than one provider (another company can own the same name), `jobs_board_candidates` lists them with their main locations, and the script picks the one whose locations match the offices in the facts file (`--offices`; give the metro area as well as the suburb, since boards write "Los Angeles, CA" for an office in Hawthorne), and puts `office_matches` on a single guessed board, with a warning when none of its roles is in an office given, or reports `jobs_board_ambiguous`: rerun with `provider:token` for the company's own board. When the careers page refuses the script (`jobs_page_unreadable`), `jobs` still tries boards named after the domain; do not fetch the page another way. `jobs_board_unsupported` names an applicant tracking system the command cannot read (Comeet, common at Israeli companies, Workable, Workday, and others): count the open roles on the careers page by hand if it opens in a browser, and say under Gaps that no trend was measured. In step 4, compare both with the headcount and growth claims.

### US federal money, run by you

When the entity record has a US entity, run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py usaspending "<legal name>" "<US entity name>" ... --since <founding year>-01-01 > "<run folder>/usaspending.jsonl"` while the agents work, with the legal names (former names too; leave off "Inc." and other suffixes). It reads USAspending.gov's open API for the last five years (from `--since`, the founding year; loans are searched back to April 2020 in any case, so PPP loans show): the largest contracts, grants, direct payments, other assistance, and loans to recipients whose names contain every distinctive word of a name (`usa_award`, with the agency, amount, dates, description, and award page), each fiscal year's obligations for the recipients found (`usa_year`), and a `usa_summary` with each recipient's UEI and page, each agency's share of the listed awards, any truncated groups, and the names the search returned that did not match. Check each recipient's page (address, parent) before counting it as the company. `usa_vehicles` lists the indefinite-delivery contracts (IDIQs) the company holds a place on, which carry no money themselves; a `usa_flag` marks a fiscal year with negative obligations (an agency took money back: open the largest award's transaction history to see whether it was a closeout, a cut in scope, or a termination). USAspending leaves out Other Transaction agreements (DIU and other OT deals, often a defence startup's largest) and some SBIR awards, so add those from SBIR.gov's firm portfolio (`https://www.sbir.gov/portfolio/<id>`, which the Startup agent finds) and the company's contract announcements, and say which figures come from where. In step 4, put the federal total and the largest agency under Key facts, and compare it with the revenue in Financials: a federal share of revenue above about a third is a concentration to state in Key facts, not a red flag. Loans include SBA's PPP and disaster loans; a PPP loan is a fact about 2020 and 2021, not income. If the script fails, say so under Gaps.

### GLEIF ownership, run by you

You already ran `mr.py gleif <LEI>` in step 1. Run it also for each parent, acquisition-company, and acquired-subsidiary name in the entity record that has an LEI of its own (`mr.py gleif "<name>"` finds it), since a group's holding companies often have one when the operating company does not. Write `<run folder>/gleif.md` from the records, in the brief's report format, working through the checks in [gleif.md](references/sources/gleif.md): the entity record, the parent chain or the `exception_reason` where it stops, the subsidiaries and their countries, and any `gleif_flag` record (an offshore parent) as a red flag quoting the parent's name and country. A `gleif_lapsed` record means the LEI is not maintained: say so under Facts, and check its legal name against the register before using it. A company with no LEI is a result: say so and list the searches you ran. If the script fails, send the GLEIF ownership agent instead.

### Debt and valuation marks, run by you

The script does this source's searching and reading, so an agent would mostly add cost. After sending the agents:

1. Collect the borrower names: the legal and trading names, former names, and the parent and holding company names in the entity record, plus the most distinctive single word of each (holders spell the same borrower differently).
2. Run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py --user-agent "<SEC User-Agent>" marks "<name>" "<word>" ... > "<run folder>/debt-and-valuation-marks.jsonl"`.
3. Look through the records' `identifier` fields (lender rows) and `name` and `title` fields (fund holdings) for borrower names you did not search, such as a fund holding titled "<company> (<acquisition company>)". Run `marks` again only for names whose distinctive word you have not already searched (a round-1 search for "Falcon" already matched every "Falcon …" vehicle; within one run the script itself skips a name a shorter one covers), appending (`>>`) to the same file with `--skip-seen "<run folder>/debt-and-valuation-marks.jsonl"`: the script also skips a name an earlier search covered (reported as `already_searched`) and leaves out records already there (`already_seen`). One extra round is enough. Drop any name `marks` reports as `generic_alias`: it is a common word, and its results are mostly unrelated. A record with `name_mismatch` was matched only by a one-word alias and lacks the rest of the borrower's name ("Papaya Growth Opportunity Corp." for "Papaya Global"): check it is the borrower before using it, and leave it out otherwise.
4. Write `<run folder>/debt-and-valuation-marks.md` from the records, in the brief's report format, working through the checks in [debt-and-valuation-marks.md](references/sources/debt-and-valuation-marks.md). Each red flag cites its filing URL and quotes the record's `quote`: a loan row as the page shows it, or a fund holding's name, balance, and value in the brief's XML form. Take each fund's trend, for shares or loans, from its `fund_mark_change` records. A loan's `flagged_footnotes` (non-accrual, PIK, an amendment, a default) come from the filing's own footnotes, and a fund holding's `fund_flags` (in default, interest in arrears, paid in kind) from the fund's own report, so neither needs further opening.

If the script fails (an `error` record or no output at all), send the Debt and valuation marks agent after all, telling it what you ran. A `no_holdings_found` record is a result, not a failure: no SEC-filing lender or fund holds these names, so say so in the report and cite the full-text searches in its `urls`. When it carries `partial: true`, some filings could not be read (its `unread` list, with an `error` record each): say so under Gaps, naming them.

If the runtime has no subagents, work through the sources yourself one at a time, following the same two files for each and writing the same report files. If it has no web access at all, stop and tell the user: this skill cannot run without it.

Done when every agent has replied, every report file exists or the agent's status line says why it does not, and you have written the reports for the sources you ran yourself (UK Companies House, GLEIF ownership, Debt and valuation marks) or sent their agents.

## 4. Write the dossier

Read every report file in the run folder before writing anything. After context compaction, read them again rather than working from a summary. When the S&P Global report says `RATINGS SEARCH: needs a browser`, open that search yourself in a browser tool (the URL is in [sp-global.md](references/sources/sp-global.md), check 1), record the rating actions as that check says (from their article pages; never open a PDF or other document link in a browser, which starts a download dialog for the user), and use them as part of the S&P findings; with no browser tool, keep it under Gaps. Then:

- **Cross-check.** Compare the facts that more than one source covers: officers and directors, registered and head office addresses, headcount (a company's filed staff count covers that legal entity only, not its subsidiaries, so compare it with a group headcount claim only when the accounts are consolidated), revenue, ownership, dates, and funding rounds the Startup or Press report claims against the share allotments and shareholder lists in the register reports. Record each disagreement in the dossier with both citations, not just the version you prefer. Funding the company itself claims that no share allotment or disclosed debt matches is a red flag, established by the register filing. An allotment that exists but is too small for the round is a conflict, not a red flag: a round of new money adds about `amount ÷ post-money valuation` to the share count, so compare that with the percentage increase in shares or nominal capital (a $150m round at $2.5bn post-money is about 6%; a 2% increase means most of the round was a sale of existing shares, or the register filing is not the whole round), and record both under Conflicts between sources. Compare layoffs (`warn_notice` records and the Press report) and the open-roles trend (`jobs_snapshot` records) with the company's headcount and growth claims: a layoff of 10% or more of its headcount in the last 12 months, or one that contradicts a growth claim made after it, is a red flag; open roles falling by more than half over 6 to 12 months, or a department's roles disappearing, go under Facts, and count as a red flag only beside a layoff or a funding gap. Compare outstanding charges in the UK register (`ch_charge` records) with the debt note in the accounts and the Financials report: a charge the accounts do not explain, such as an outstanding charge beside a statement that the company has no debt, goes under Conflicts between sources with both citations.
- **Follow up group names found late.** Reports often name group companies the entity record lacked: an ultimate parent or controlling party in a subsidiary's accounts, the entity a rating agency rates, a borrower or holding vehicle in lenders' filings. For each such name, run `mr.py gleif "<name>"` and, if it could borrow, `mr.py marks --skip-seen "<run folder>/debt-and-valuation-marks.jsonl" "<name>" "<distinctive word>" >> "<run folder>/debt-and-valuation-marks.jsonl"`, adding what they find to the GLEIF and Debt and valuation marks reports. Treat people and owners the same way: officers, directors, and legal owners that the reports name and the entity record lacked, such as the related persons in a Form D, the directors of a subsidiary found late, or the legal owners a register lists (datacvr's owners with 5% or more, for example), count as new names by default. Then, if any new name is not already screened (a spelling variant of a screened name counts as screened, and so does any name containing a distinctive word the Sanctions and offshore agent already searched in every list it used, such as "Mitnick" for every Mitnick vehicle; its report lists its searches per list), screen them yourself when there are ten or fewer, as [sanctions-offshore.md](references/sources/sanctions-offshore.md) describes: run `mr.py csl` and `mr.py icij` once each with all of them, and search each in OpenSanctions (with the API key, or its website search through the web fetch tool), and write the results to `<run folder>/late-names.md` in the brief's report format. For more names, send one agent with the Sanctions and offshore source file, those names only, and that report file. Fold the findings in either way. Holding vehicles rarely have court cases of their own, so leave courts to the main run. If you send none, list the unscreened names under Gaps.
- **Registers for group companies found late.** When reports name group companies registered outside the US and UK that GLEIF, a register, or the accounts confirm as current subsidiaries (not an acquisition named only in the press, which may have been an asset purchase) and the National registers agent was not sent, send it now for those companies, with its own report file, and fold its findings in; if you send none, list those companies under Gaps. When the country has an `mr.py` command, run it for the new company first (for Luxembourg, rerun `lu` with every group company's RCS number in one run) and give the agent the file; when a register agent is still to be sent, wait for the register agents already running to report before sending it, so one agent covers every company found in that country.
- **Merge duplicates.** When several agents report the same red flag (the same loan mark, the same rating action), keep it once and cite every filing behind it.
- **Confirm red flags.** Every red flag must be found on its cited page, saying what the agent reported, and be about this entity. Run `python <skill folder>/scripts/mr.py --user-agent "<SEC User-Agent>" verify-report "<run folder>"`: it reads every report's red flags, fetches each cited page once (filings of tens of megabytes included), and returns one `red_flag_check` per flag with its `result`:
  - `confirmed`: the quote is on the page; read the returned `context` to check it says what the agent reported and is about this entity. A `warnings` entry means the quote is too short to carry the flagged figures, or appears more than once so the context may show another row or period: check that flag with `quote-check` and a fuller quote;
  - `unreachable`: no cited page could be fetched, usually a site that refuses scripts such as spglobal.com; check the quote in a browser tool;
  - `js_page`: the cited page is built by JavaScript (a register app such as KvK's), so its data is not in the fetched HTML; check the quote in a browser tool;
  - `not_found`: the page loaded but the quote is not on it; open the page yourself, and if it does not say what the agent reported, move the flag to Gaps;
  - `no_text_layer`: the cited page is a PDF with no text to search, often a scan; read it yourself;
  - `no_quote` or `no_url`: the agent left the quote or URL out; find the evidence in the report or move the flag to Gaps.

  `mr.py quote-check <url> "<quote>"` checks a single quote the same way. Without Python, open each cited page yourself and search it for the quote; for a large filing, download it, strip the markup, and search the text for the quote's distinctive values, such as the borrower name and the fair value. Move a red flag you cannot confirm to Gaps with the reason.
- **Rank red flags.** Rank the confirmed red flags, most serious first, judged by the likely effect on the company's ability to keep operating and pay its debts: insolvency, default, a sanctions listing, a CCC-range rating, or loans valued far below par usually come before enforcement actions, litigation, overdue filings, auditor changes or qualified accounts, ownership hidden behind offshore or nominee layers, and adverse press. For each one, say whether it is an allegation, a filed claim, or an established finding (a fact on the record from the body that made it: a court judgment, a regulator's decision, a company filing, a rating agency's action, or a lender's or fund's filed valuation).
- **List gaps.** Name every source that failed or found nothing, and what it could not cover.

Use this structure:

```markdown
# <Legal name> — muckraker dossier (<date>)

## Entity
Legal name, jurisdiction, identifiers, website, parent, one-paragraph summary of what the company does.

## Red flags
Ranked, each with status (allegation / filed claim / established) and citations. "None found" is a valid entry.

## Financials
The multi-year table and derived figures from the Financials report, then debt (lenders, maturities, marks) and valuation marks. Cited.

## Key facts
People in company roles, ownership, filings, headcount, ratings, cases, recent events. Cited.

## Conflicts between sources
Each disagreement with both citations.

## Gaps
Sources that failed, returned nothing, or were paywalled, and what that leaves unknown.

## Sources
Every URL cited, grouped under a bold line per source, one `- [Title or site, date](url)` per URL.
```

Write every link as standard markdown, so it works in any viewer and when the dossier is shown in the chat:

- **Citations** in the body are inline links with short text, such as `([Companies House filing](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/09677925/filing-history))`. Do not use labels such as `[NR1]` or `[cro-co]` that point to the Sources section: without a matching definition line they are plain text, and definition lines do not display.
- **Sources** lists each URL as a visible inline link, as in the structure above. No bare URLs, no `[label]: url` definition lines, and no links to the report files in place of URLs (the run folder path, given after the dossier, covers those).
- **Characters:** percent-encode spaces, double quotes, braces, and parentheses in a URL (`%20`, `%22`, `%7B`, `%7D`, `%28`, `%29`), as in `https://www.thegazette.co.uk/insolvency/notice?text=%22Snyk%22`; unencoded, they break the link.

Write one dossier per company and save it as `<run folder>/dossier.md`. Show the dossier in the chat, and give the run folder's path so the user can read it and the full source reports.

Done when every claim in the dossier has a citation, from an agent's report or from a page you opened yourself in this step, every chosen source appears under its findings or under Gaps, every link is an inline markdown link as above, and the dossier is saved and shown.
